What would happen if you trained like a Dragon Ball fighter? Gravity rooms. Punches that shake mountains. Flying. Energy blasts. Today we find out how much of that a real human body could actually do... and the answer at the end might surprise you. Let's go.

Chapter one: the gravity room. In the show, fighters train at a hundred times Earth's gravity. So what happens if you step inside? At a hundred g, a seventy kilogram person weighs seven tonnes. That's heavier than an elephant. You couldn't stand up. You couldn't even lift your arm, because your arm alone would weigh about three hundred and fifty kilograms. But the real killer is your blood. Your heart is built to push blood up to your brain against one g. Crank the gravity up, and your blood sinks into your legs. Most untrained people black out somewhere around four to six g. Fighter pilots, with special suits and breathing tricks, can handle about nine.

So what's the most g a human has ever survived on purpose? Take a guess... In 1954, an Air Force doctor rode a rocket sled that stopped so hard he felt about forty-six g. Forty-six! But only for a split second, and his eyes filled with blood and he was blinded for a while. A hundred g, for hours of push-ups? That's not training. That's a pancake.

Chapter two: how strong can a human get? Dragon Ball fighters toss boulders the size of houses. The real deadlift world record is about five hundred and eleven kilograms. That's like lifting a whole horse. Incredible... but a house-sized boulder weighs thousands of tonnes. And muscle has a limit. To get stronger, you need bigger muscles, and bigger muscles are heavier to carry around. There's a ceiling, and the strongest humans are already close to it.

Chapter three: the punch. An elite boxer's fist flies at about ten meters per second, and can hit with around five thousand newtons. That's like half a tonne slamming into you for an instant. Want to know who has the fastest punch on Earth? It's not a human. Guess... It's the mantis shrimp. Its club strikes at about twenty-three meters per second, so fast that the water rips open into tiny bubbles, that collapse with a bang and hit the prey a second time. And to punch at the speed of sound, like in the show? Your fist would need to go about thirty-four times faster than a boxer's. Your arm would be torn apart long before that.

Chapter four: could you fly? To hover, you have to push the air down with a force equal to your whole weight. Birds do it with giant chest muscles... but the heaviest flying birds top out at around twenty kilograms. You're more than three times heavier. In 2013, engineers built a pedal-powered helicopter. Its rotors spread as wide as a football field, the pilot was a super-fit cyclist... and it hovered for sixty-four seconds, about three meters off the ground. That's the best human muscles have ever done.

Chapter five: the energy blast. Where would all that energy even come from? Here's the wild part: you're already carrying it. An average adult has around fifteen kilograms of body fat, and fat is packed with energy, about nine calories in every gram. Add it up, and that's roughly the same energy as one hundred and thirty-five kilograms of TNT. If you could release it all at once, that would be one serious blast. You just can't. Your body burns it slowly, to keep you alive.

But wait... it gets crazier. Einstein's famous equation, E equals m c squared, says that mass itself is energy. If every bit of your body turned into pure energy, you'd release about one thousand five hundred megatons. That's thirty times the biggest bomb ever tested. So could you blow up a planet? Guess how many of you it would take to blow the Earth apart... About thirty-five trillion. Even fully converted, you are not a planet buster.

So, what would happen if you trained like a Dragon Ball fighter? The gravity room would flatten you. Your best punch tops out around half a tonne. Flying takes a machine as wide as a football field. And the energy is inside you... but it's locked away. The good news? Real training still works. Lift, run, sleep and eat well, and your body adapts to almost anything you throw at it. Just maybe skip the hundred times gravity part.
